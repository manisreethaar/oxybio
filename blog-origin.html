The 3 AM realisation: why India's health drink market needed a different question

The moment building a functional food company stopped being a business idea and became something I had to do.

It was 3:00 AM on a Tuesday, and I was staring at a spreadsheet that broke my understanding of the Indian health market. For weeks, I had been auditing the nutritional profiles of the best-selling protein powders, malt drinks and health foods in the country.

The numbers made perfect commercial sense. They made much less nutritional sense.

The illusion of nutrition

As a science-trained founder, you are taught to look for inefficiencies in markets. What I found looked more like a settled compromise. Many of the products families trust are built on sugar and maltodextrin, with a long list of added vitamins on the back label.

A long list on a label tells you what went into the tin. It does not tell you what a person's body actually receives. That gap, between what is printed and what is measured, is where this company started.

We achieved incredible distribution scale in India. We forgot to distribute evidence.

The turning point

That night, the premise for Oxygen Bioinnovations was born. I did not want to build another D2C brand playing the marketing game on Instagram. I wanted to build everyday nutrition designed around the Indian diet, and to measure it before making any claim about it.

We would not use maltodextrin fillers. We would start from indigenous Indian grains, foods that have always been part of how India eats, and build the product around them with the same rigour you would expect of a laboratory.

It sounded like a strong thesis. Then I started talking to contract manufacturers, and the real difficulty began.
